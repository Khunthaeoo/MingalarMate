Daily Life & Settlement

Living in Taiwan

Practical day-to-day advice for international students: securing your ARC, enrolling in health insurance, navigating public transit, choosing accommodation, and managing daily essentials.

📅 Last reviewed: November 2026 • Topic: Practical Settlement
📋

Official Procedures vs. General Advice

Immigration registration (ARC) and National Health Insurance (NHI) enrollment are regulated by Taiwan law and have strict legal deadlines. Day-to-day tips such as choosing mobile carriers or finding off-campus rentals are general practical recommendations.

1. Immediate Arrival Essentials: Official Requirements

Upon arriving in Taiwan, completing your statutory legal registrations should be your highest priority:

Alien Resident Certificate (ARC)

International students entering Taiwan on a Resident Visa must apply for an Alien Resident Certificate (ARC) through the National Immigration Agency (NIA) within the statutory window after arrival (or switch from a Visitor Visa once enrolled).

  • Managed by the National Immigration Agency (NIA).
  • Requires your passport, visa, proof of enrollment, lease agreement/dorm proof, and photo.
  • Applications are often coordinated via your university’s international office or submitted online via the NIA portal.

National Health Insurance (NHI)

Taiwan has one of the world's most comprehensive universal healthcare systems. International students holding an ARC are legally required to join the National Health Insurance (NHI) system once eligible.

  • Students typically qualify after 6 consecutive months of residence in Taiwan (or as arranged by school policies).
  • Universities assist students in enrolling; monthly premiums are typically added to semester tuition bills.
  • Prior to NHI eligibility, students must maintain international student medical insurance (often provided through school registration).

2. Mobile SIM Cards & Student Banking

SIM Cards & Connectivity

Taiwan boasts high-speed 4G and 5G networks across the island.

  • Airport Starter SIM: You can buy a 15-day or 30-day prepaid SIM with unlimited data at Taoyuan (TPE), Songshan (TSA), or Kaohsiung (KHH) airport terminals upon arrival using your passport.
  • Student Monthly Plans: Major telecom providers (Chunghwa Telecom, Taiwan Mobile, FarEasTone) offer affordable monthly student contracts once you have your official ARC and student ID card.
  • Free Public Wi-Fi: Campus networks (Eduroam) and public city networks (iTaiwan) are widely available.

Opening a Local Bank Account

Having a local Taiwanese bank or Post Office (Chunghwa Post) account is necessary for receiving scholarship stipends, paying tuition, or getting part-time wages.

  • Universities coordinate group bank account opening sessions for new international students during orientation week.
  • Required documents typically include: Original Passport, valid ARC card, Student ID card, and a personal name stamp (chop/seal), which can be carved in Taiwan for a nominal fee.

4. Getting Around Taiwan

Taiwan features clean, safe, punctual, and highly integrated public transportation systems:

EasyCard & iPass

Rechargeable contactless IC smart cards used nationwide for metros, city buses, trains, convenience store purchases, and public bike rentals. Many university student IDs double as an EasyCard with student discounts.

MRT & City Buses

Extensive rapid transit networks operate in Taipei/New Taipei, Taoyuan, Taichung, and Kaohsiung. Clean and on time, with bilingual English signage and announcements throughout.

Railways & YouBike

Taiwan High Speed Rail (THSR) connects the west coast at speeds over 300 km/h. Taiwan Railways (TRA) circles the entire island. YouBike public shared bicycles offer convenient first-and-last-mile connectivity.

5. Everyday Life & Convenience

The Convenience Store Ecosystem

Convenience stores (7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Hi-Life, OK Mart) are integral to life in Taiwan. In addition to food and coffee, you can:

  • Pay tuition bills, utility invoices, and phone bills.
  • Collect and ship online packages and mail.
  • Use ATMs, photo printers, and copy machines.
  • Reload your EasyCard or purchase train tickets.

Safety & Natural Preparedness

Taiwan is widely recognized as one of the safest societies in the world for international travelers and solo students.

  • Emergency Numbers: Police (110), Fire & Ambulance (119).
  • Earthquake Awareness: Taiwan experiences seismic activity; familiarize yourself with the "Drop, Cover, and Hold On" drill.
  • Typhoon Season: Usually between July and October. Follow government advisories for school cancellation announcements (停班停課).

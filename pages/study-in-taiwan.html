Academic Planning Guide

Study in Taiwan

A starter guide to navigating Taiwan higher education: discovering degree programs, preparing admissions materials, understanding tuition structures, and exploring scholarship avenues.

📅 Last reviewed: November 2026 • Target: Prospective & incoming international students
💡

Verify Individual University Requirements

Admission rules, specific program prerequisites, application deadlines, and scholarship funding vary significantly across universities and degree levels in Taiwan. Always confirm exact details directly via the official admission portal of each target university or the Ministry of Education.

1. Higher Education in Taiwan: An Overview

Taiwan is renowned for academic excellence, cutting-edge research facilities, affordable tuition, and safe campus environments. Its university system primarily comprises:

Comprehensive Universities

Offer extensive academic programs across humanities, social sciences, medicine, law, natural sciences, and business. Examples include National Taiwan University (NTU), National Cheng Kung University (NCKU), and National Tsing Hua University (NTHU).

Universities of Science & Technology

Focus heavily on applied sciences, engineering, digital technology, management, and industrial partnerships. Examples include National Taiwan University of Science and Technology (Taiwan Tech) and National Taipei University of Technology (Taipei Tech).

Both English-taught programs and Chinese-taught programs are available at undergraduate and graduate levels. Prospective students can search accredited international degree programs via the official Study in Taiwan Portal ↗.

2. Admission Cycles & Preparation Timeline

Most Taiwan universities operate on two primary admission cycles:

Intake Semester Typical Application Period Semester Start Notes
Fall Intake (Primary) January – March / April September Offers the widest selection of programs and scholarship quotas.
Spring Intake (Secondary) August – October / November February Common for graduate degrees; select undergraduate programs participate.

* Note: Exact deadlines differ by institution. Some universities have multiple rounds. Check early to ensure ample time for document legalization.

3. Document Preparation Checklist

While each department specifies exact submissions, international applicants generally need:

  • Completed Online Application Form: Submitted through the university's international admission portal.
  • Official Academic Transcripts & Diplomas: Authenticated/legalized by the appropriate Taipei Economic and Cultural Representative Office (TECO) or designated overseas representative office.
  • Proof of Language Proficiency:
    • For English-taught programs: TOEFL, IELTS, TOEIC, or institutional English-medium instruction certificates as specified.
    • For Chinese-taught programs: TOCFL (Test of Chinese as a Foreign Language) score certificate at the required level (usually Level 2 to Level 4).
  • Financial Statement / Proof of Funds: Bank certificate showing sufficient funds to cover initial tuition and living expenses, or an official scholarship award letter.
  • Study Plan or Statement of Purpose (SOP): Outlining your academic background, research interests, and rationale for choosing Taiwan.
  • Letters of Recommendation: Usually 1 to 2 letters from previous professors, teachers, or supervisors.
  • Copy of Valid Passport: Valid for at least six months into the planned study period.

4. Scholarships & Tuition Planning

Taiwan is often recognized for reasonable tuition rates compared to Western study destinations, with extensive scholarship programs to attract global talent.

Taiwan MOE Scholarship

Funded by the Ministry of Education. Provides tuition support plus a monthly living stipend for qualified degree-seeking students. Applications are usually submitted via local TECO offices between February and March.

University Scholarships

Almost all public and private universities offer direct merit-based institutional scholarships, such as full tuition waivers, partial tuition discounts, or monthly teaching/research assistant stipends.

TaiwanICDF Scholarship

Offered by the International Cooperation and Development Fund for students from partner countries enrolled in specific designated graduate degree programs.

Part-time Jobs in Taiwan

Essential guidelines on work permit regulations, legal work hour limits, the application workflow through EZ Work Taiwan, and how to evaluate job opportunities responsibly.

📅 Last reviewed: November 2026 • Compliance: Ministry of Labor Regulations
⚠️

Critical Legal Requirement: Work Permit Mandatory

Under the Employment Service Act of Taiwan, international students must not engage in any paid or unpaid employment without holding a valid Work Permit issued by the Ministry of Labor. Working without a permit is illegal and carries severe consequences, including hefty statutory fines, immediate permit revocation, and deportation with reentry restrictions.

Disclaimer: MingalarMate does not provide individualized legal or immigration advice and cannot guarantee work eligibility. Always consult your university’s Office of International Affairs (OIA) and the Ministry of Labor.

1. The Legal Framework: What Every Student Must Know

Taiwan welcomes international talent, but maintains strict regulations to ensure international students prioritize their primary purpose of education:

Valid Work Permit Required

Every international student must possess a physical or electronic Work Permit issued by the Workforce Development Agency (WDA) before their very first day of employment.

Maximum 20 Hours / Week

During regular school semesters, international students are permitted to work a maximum of 20 hours per week. During official summer and winter vacations, this limit is lifted in accordance with current Ministry of Labor provisions.

Penalties for Illegal Work

Working without a valid permit or exceeding allowed hours violates Article 43 and Article 68 of the Employment Service Act. Violators face fines of NT$30,000 to NT$150,000, order to leave Taiwan, and loss of student visa status.

2. Step-by-Step Work Permit Application Process

Work permit applications for foreign students are processed online through the EZ Work Taiwan government platform:

  1. 1

    Verify Eligibility with Your University

    Ensure you are formally enrolled in a recognized degree program or accredited Chinese language center. You must possess a valid passport, valid Alien Resident Certificate (ARC), and a current semester enrollment stamp on your student ID.

  2. 2

    Register an Account on EZ Work Taiwan

    Visit the official EZ Work Taiwan Portal ↗. Create a student account using your ARC number, passport details, and active email address.

  3. 3

    Upload Required Documentation

    Prepare clear PDF/image scans of your ARC card (front and back), valid student ID showing current semester registration, and proof of statutory examination fee payment (NT$100, payable at Post Office or convenience store ATM).

  4. 4

    School Review & Official Approval

    Your university’s Office of International Affairs (OIA) first endorses your application online. Upon university endorsement, the Workforce Development Agency reviews and issues your official Work Permit (typically valid for up to 6 months per semester).

3. Exploring Safe Part-time Opportunities

When seeking employment, prioritize roles that support your academic growth, language learning, and personal safety:

On-Campus Employment (Highly Recommended)

On-campus opportunities are convenient, understand academic calendar constraints, and ensure labor compliance. Common options include:

  • Academic Department Assistants: Helping faculty with course materials, laboratories, or administrative tasks.
  • Campus Library / Computer Center Staff: Managing checkouts, cataloging, and equipment assistance.
  • Office of International Affairs (OIA) Student Ambassador: Assisting new incoming international students during orientations and events.
  • Language Exchange & Tutoring: Peer tutoring in English or native language instruction.

Off-Campus Employment

If working off campus, choose reputable, registered businesses that respect statutory minimum wage laws and work hour limits:

  • Food & Beverage / Cafes: Baristas, bakery assistants, restaurant waitstaff in student neighborhoods.
  • Language Services & Translation: Document translation, transcription, or business communication.
  • Retail & Hospitality: Bookstores, convenience stores, and hotel front-desk bilingual support.

4. Protecting Yourself: Safety Checklist & Red Flags

International students must stay vigilant against unethical practices or fraudulent job advertisements. Watch out for these warning signs:

  • Never Surrender Original Documents: Legitimate employers will examine your ARC and Work Permit, but they will never confiscate or retain your original passport, ARC, bank passbook, or ATM card.
  • Refuse Upfront Payment Demands: Never pay "training fees", "deposit fees", or "uniform fees" upfront to secure a position.
  • Beware of Vague Job Descriptions: Be skeptical of social media advertisements promising unrealistically high wages for "simple package handling" or "money transfers"—these frequently involve illegal financial scam syndicates.
  • Demand Formal Payslips & Minimum Wage Compliance: Ensure your hourly rate complies with the statutory national basic wage announced by the Ministry of Labor.
